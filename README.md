- Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC)
- Pima Indians Diabetes
- Heart Disease
- Parkinson’s Disease

The proposed NEFT-Md framework was experimentally evaluated using four heterogeneous benchmark datasets frequently employed in uncertainty-aware medical classification and machine learning research. The datasets were selected to represent different geometric structures, uncertainty distributions, and boundary complexities.

The Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset contains diagnostic measurements extracted from digitized breast mass images. The features describe geometric and statistical properties of cell nuclei, including radius, texture, perimeter, area, smoothness, compactness, concavity, and symmetry. The classification task aims to distinguish between benign and malignant tumors.

The Pima Indians Diabetes dataset contains physiological and medical diagnostic measurements associated with diabetes prediction, including glucose concentration, blood pressure, body mass index (BMI), insulin levels, skin thickness, and age. This dataset is characterized by elevated uncertainty propagation and nonlinear feature overlap.

The Heart Disease dataset contains clinical measurements related to cardiovascular disease diagnosis, including chest pain type, cholesterol level, resting blood pressure, maximum heart rate, electrocardiographic attributes, and exercise-induced angina. The dataset presents irregular neighborhood structures and moderate class overlap.

The Parkinson’s Disease dataset contains biomedical voice measurements associated with Parkinson’s disease detection. The extracted attributes include vocal frequency perturbation measures, shimmer, jitter, nonlinear dynamical characteristics, and vocal stability descriptors. This dataset exhibits elevated structural variability and uncertainty-aware classification complexity.

# File 2 (Neutrosophic Topological Benchmark Data)

This file represents the neutrosophic-topological transformation of four heterogeneous benchmark medical datasets used throughout the proposed NEFT-Md framework. The original datasets include the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC), Pima Indians Diabetes, Heart Disease, and Parkinson’s Disease datasets. Each dataset was transformed into a neutrosophic topological representation in order to preserve uncertainty-aware geometric relationships, multiscale neighborhood structures, and boundary instability characteristics during classification.

Unlike conventional numerical datasets, the transformed neutrosophic-topological representation incorporates truth, indeterminacy, and falsity membership structures associated with each sample and feature. This transformation enables the framework to model ambiguous, inconsistent, and partially overlapping data distributions more effectively than traditional statistical or machine learning representations.

The neutrosophic transformation process was performed using the proposed Md-variance weighted uncertainty modeling procedure. Initially, all datasets were normalized and weighted according to their mutual dependency and variance behavior. The transformed weighted features were subsequently mapped into neutrosophic membership structures capable of preserving uncertainty propagation and local structural instability.

After neutrosophic transformation, multiscale filtration analysis and topology construction were applied to preserve neighborhood consistency across different geometric scales. The proposed topology-aware architecture further incorporated the Boundary Instability Index (BII) to identify structurally unstable transition regions and perform localized boundary refinement near uncertain classification areas.

The transformed neutrosophic-topological datasets were subsequently used to conduct extensive experimental analysis, including predictive classification evaluation, comparative statistical analysis, sensitivity analysis, ablation studies, and computational scalability assessment across all benchmark datasets.

1. Data Structure:
The original benchmark datasets contain heterogeneous medical and biomedical measurements associated with tumor diagnosis, diabetes prediction, cardiovascular disease detection, and Parkinson’s disease analysis. The neutrosophic-topological transformation converts these conventional feature spaces into uncertainty-aware multiscale structures capable of preserving local geometric consistency and boundary-aware neighborhood relationships.

2. Uncertainty-Aware Representation:
Unlike traditional machine learning representations that rely exclusively on precise numerical measurements, the proposed neutrosophic framework preserves truth, indeterminacy, and falsity membership structures for each feature and sample. This enables the framework to model incomplete, ambiguous, and nonlinear uncertainty distributions frequently observed in real-world medical classification environments.

3. Multiscale Topological Modeling:
The proposed framework applies multiscale filtration and topology construction mechanisms to preserve neighborhood stability across varying geometric scales. This hierarchical topological representation improves structural discrimination near complex transition regions and reduces instability caused by irregular class overlap.

4. Boundary Instability Analysis:
The proposed Boundary Instability Index (BII) identifies structurally unstable samples located near uncertain class boundaries. The resulting topology-aware refinement mechanism improves neighborhood coherence and preserves stable classification behavior under heterogeneous uncertainty conditions.

5. Comparative Experimental Analysis:
The transformed neutrosophic-topological datasets were evaluated using extensive experimental procedures involving classification performance analysis, comparative statistical evaluation against competing machine learning and uncertainty-aware models, confusion matrix analysis, sensitivity analysis, ablation studies, and scalability assessment.

6. Robustness and Scalability:
The experimental observations demonstrate that the proposed NEFT-Md framework preserves stable predictive behavior, statistically significant performance improvements, and scalable computational growth across all benchmark datasets despite substantial differences in uncertainty propagation, structural variability, and geometric complexity.

Prior to topology construction and multiscale filtration analysis, all datasets were transformed into neutrosophic representations using the proposed Md-variance weighted uncertainty modeling procedure, allowing the framework to preserve truth, indeterminacy, and falsity membership structures during topology-aware classification.
